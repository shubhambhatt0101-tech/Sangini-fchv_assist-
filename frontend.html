The Problem

Frontline health workers carry the system, but lack the tools.

Over 52,000 FCHVs in Nepal are the first point of contact for rural maternal and child health. They work without digital records, without follow-up systems, and without a structured way to hand patients to the next level of care.

Source: PMC / NCBI, published FCHV study, 2019

Fragmented patient memory

No reliable record of a mother or child’s history across visits. Everything depends on paper registers and personal memory.

FCHVs Across Nepal

Missed follow-ups

Pregnant women miss antenatal visits. Children miss vaccination schedules. There is no automated tracking or reminder system at field level.

The Human Cost

When continuity breaks, the journey to care starts too late.

These are not edge cases. In Nepal’s Far-Western hills, mothers still deliver on the road to the health post. The gap is not awareness; it is a missing thread of tracking, risk recognition and coordinated referral.

The Insight

The system is not missing data.
It is missing continuity.

Data exists in paper registers, in memory, in scattered notes and health cards. But nothing follows the patient across time, across visits, across providers. Every new encounter starts from zero.

Sangini is built to fix the continuity gap, not to add another form to fill in.

About Us

Built for the women who carry the system.

Sangini is a field health system for Nepal’s 52,000+ Female Community Health Volunteers. It turns a spoken visit into a structured record that follows the patient from the village to the health facility, even without internet access.

We presented Sangini at TMSoN 2026, the National Telemedicine & Digital Health Conference. It is in pre-pilot development, and we are looking for NGOs and health programmes to co-develop a pilot.

Voice first

FCHVs speak naturally. Sangini records the visit, so there is no extra form to fill in.

Works offline

Records stay on the device and sync when a connection returns.

Continuity of care

A patient’s history travels with her across visits and providers.
